# ECE 6900: Secure AI-Driven Energy Applications 

This was a special topics course for Spring 2026 and will contain all work done in this course. This is a group-project based course where most of the work revolves around my group team members and myself. Note, this is @ Tennessee Tech. Earned an A in this course.

**Project Abstract**: Modern residential energy systems increasingly incorporate distributed energy resources, rooftop photovoltaics, and residential battery storage; hence, creating both the opportunity and the need for intelligent, coordinated demand response mechanisms. Virtual power plants aggregate these resources to provide grid services at scale, yet existing approaches rarely address AI-driven decision logic, data integrity verification, and prosumers privacy within a single unified system. This paper presents Voltwatch, a privacy-preserving and AI-assisted virtual power plant monitoring proof-of-concept grounded in the StoreNet Irish residential energy community dataset, a real-world collection of one-minute resolution power, energy, and meteorological measurements from 20 consumer households over 2020. Voltwatch implements a five-layer modular pipeline that spans data ingestion, signal processing, RNN-based load forecasting, integrity-verified cyber-validation, and live dashboard visualization. An empirical model selection study comparing five candidate architectures identifies the RNN as the best-performing forecaster on aggregated community demand. The system operates exclusively on community-level aggregated demand profiles rather than individual household data, a design choice jointly motivated by forecasting accuracy and re-identification risk. Community-level MAPE is approximately 8\% versus 40\%+ for individual households, and as few as five consecutive households in large-scale datasets. A lightweight cyber-validation layer combines hash-based data integrity verification with threshold-based false data injection detection, providing interpretable security without the complexity of full cryptographic or federated learning frameworks. The results demonstrate that interdisciplinary integration of AI, cybersecurity, and energy system principles can be achieved within a coherent, educationally transparent simulation architecture grounded in peer-reviewed design justifications.

**Course Description**: This interdisciplinary course explores the intersection of Artificial Intelligence, cybersecurity, and 
energy systems, equipping students with both foundational knowledge and convergence insights. 
Participants will study core concepts of AI and machine learning, principles of cybersecurity, and 
the modern energy systems, including smart grids and renewable integration. Through case studies, 
group-based hands-on projects, and research-driven discussions, students will gain the skills to 
analyze, design, and secure AI-driven energy applications. Students will work in team and propose 
a semester long project integrating the technologies in AI, cybersecurity and energy. Key 
milestones will be defined by the team, and capstone-style final project presentations will be 
required.
